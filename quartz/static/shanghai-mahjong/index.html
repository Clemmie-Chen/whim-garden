胡 四組一對　即可胡牌 麻將

Four sets,
one pair.

Collect four sets and one pair before anyone else, and you win. Every other rule in Shanghai mahjong builds on that one idea.

麻

What mahjong is

Four players sit around a square table. Instead of cards there are tiles. On your turn you draw one tile and discard one, slowly shaping your hand into sets.

If you have played Rummy, you already know the feeling.

In Shanghai people say 搓麻将 cuō májiàng, “rubbing mahjong”, after the sound of tiles being shuffled by hand.

牌

The tiles

Forget the full set for now. Start with three suits, each numbered 1 to 9, four copies of every tile: 108 in all.

Dots筒 tǒng

Count the circles.

Bamboo条 tiáo

Count the sticks. On a real set, the 1 is a bird.

Characters万 wàn

The top symbol is a Chinese number, one to nine.

組

Sets and pairs

Everything you build is one of three shapes.

Run顺子 shùnzi

Three in a row, in one suit.

Triplet刻子 kèzi

Three identical tiles.

Pair对子 duìzi

Two identical tiles.

A run never mixes suits, and it never wraps around: 8, 9, 1 is nothing.

試

Run, triplet, or neither?

Four groups of three. Name each one.

胡

The winning hand

A winning hand is four sets and one pair: 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 2 = 14 tiles. Runs and triplets mix freely.

Not yet

Swap one tile and the hand falls apart. 3, 4, 6 of Bamboo skips a number, so it is not a run.

聽

One tile away

You hold 13 tiles. When a single tile would finish the hand you are ready, 听牌 tīngpái. This hand is ready. Which tile lets you call Hú?

摸

Draw one, discard one

  1. You hold 13 tiles.
  2. On your turn, draw one from the wall. Now you have 14.
  3. If they form four sets and a pair, call Hú! 胡 You win.
  4. If not, discard one tile face up. Play moves to the player on your right.

Your first game

You can play right now with a simple version. Use only the 108 number tiles. Everyone takes 13; the dealer takes 14 and discards first. Keep all hands face up so everyone learns from everyone, and draw only from the wall. If the wall runs out before anyone wins, deal again.

風

The full set

A Shanghai set has 144 tiles: the 108 number tiles plus 36 more.

Winds

East, South, West, North. 16 tiles. Triplets and pairs only, never runs.

Dragons

Red, Green, White. 12 tiles. In Shanghai they count as flowers.

Flowers and seasons

8 bonus tiles, never part of your hand.

Draw a flower, lay it face up, and take a replacement from the back of the wall. This is bǔhuā 补花. Flowers do not finish your hand, but they make a win worth more.

莊

Starting a hand

  1. Roll dice to choose the first dealer, 庄家 zhuāngjiā.
  2. Build the wall. Most Shanghai mahjong rooms have automatic tables that shuffle and build it for you.
  3. The dealer takes 14 tiles. Everyone else takes 13.
  4. Starting with the dealer, everyone sets aside their flowers and draws replacements.
  5. The dealer discards first. Play moves to the right.
  6. Whoever wins becomes the next dealer. If nobody wins, the dealer stays.
碰

Taking discards

You can also take a tile someone throws away, if it completes a set. The price: that set goes face up on the table and can never change.

  • Pung 碰 pèngfrom anyoneYou hold a pair, and someone throws the third.
  • Chow 吃 chīfrom your left onlyYou hold two tiles of a run, and the missing one is thrown.
  • Kong 杠 gàngfour identicalExposed: you hold three and someone throws the fourth. Concealed: you draw all four. Added: you ponged three, then draw the fourth. A kong is one set, so always draw a replacement.

A tile hits the table. Who threw it?

You

Pick a seat to see what you may call.

Say the call out loud, take the tile, lay the set face up, then discard. If two players want the same tile, a win beats a pung or kong, and a pung or kong beats a chow.

贏

Two ways to win

  • Self-draw 自摸 zìmōall three payYou draw the winning tile yourself.
  • Win on a discard 点炮 diǎnpàothe thrower paysSomeone throws your winning tile.

So every discard has two jobs: improve your hand, and avoid handing someone theirs. Tiles already in the discard pile are usually the safest to throw.

One full turn

  1. Draw a tile. If it is a flower, show it and draw again.
  2. If you can win, call Hú! The hand is over.
  3. If you want to declare a kong, show it, draw a replacement, and go back to step 2.
  4. Discard one tile.
  5. The others react: someone may win on it, pung or kong it, or (the next player only) chow it. If nobody wants it, the next player draws.
敲

Knock before you win

The most popular style in Shanghai today is Qiao Ma, 敲麻 qiāo má, “knock mahjong”. It adds three things: you must knock before you can win, flowers decide how much you win, and every hand has a payout cap.

When you are one tile from winning, announce qiāo! 敲 and stand one tile up as a signal.

  • No knock, no win. Self-draws and discards alike. The only exception is a dealer who wins on the very first 14 tiles.
  • Your hand is frozen. Any tile you draw that does not win must be thrown away.
  • You must take the win. If someone throws your winning tile, you cannot wait for a bigger one.
  • Missed win. Let a winning tile pass, and you cannot win on that same tile until after your next draw.

Knocking tells the whole table you are close. Everyone starts throwing safe tiles and the room goes quiet. That tension is the heart of Shanghai mahjong.

花

Counting flowers

In Qiao Ma a flower, 花 huā, is a scoring point. You earn them from:

  • Each flower or season tile1
  • Each dragon tile 中 發 白1
  • A triplet of winds1
  • A kong of winds, exposed / concealed2 / 3
  • A kong of number tiles, exposed / concealed1 / 2

To win a plain hand on someone’s discard you need at least one flower. A self-drawn win needs none. Collect all eight flower and season tiles and you win on the spot.

番

Scoring

Two numbers decide a win: your flowers, and your hand’s multiplier, fān 番. The total is capped by the lèzi 勒子, often 20 points.

On a self-draw each of the three other players pays that amount. On a discard win only the thrower pays.

    The rule of thumb: the purer the hand, the bigger the win. There is no single official rulebook, so Shanghai players agree on flowers, the cap and whether chow is allowed before the first hand.

    桌

    At the table

    • Say your call out loud before you take a tile: Pèng! Chī! Gàng! Qiāo! Hú!
    • A tile that leaves your hand is discarded. You cannot take it back.
    • Keep your discards in neat rows, and never touch another player’s tiles.
    • Don’t rush other players or comment on their hands.

    Tips for your first games

    • Throw lonely winds, 1s and 9s early. Middle tiles, 3 to 7, connect more easily.
    • Two tiles side by side (4 and 5) beat two with a gap (4 and 6). The first waits for two tiles, the second for one.
    • Don’t claim everything. Every claim makes your hand less flexible.
    • Once someone knocks, throw tiles that are already in the discard pile.

    Two other Shanghai styles

    Wild card 百搭 bǎidā: a tile is flipped at the start and the next one in sequence becomes wild. Usually you can only win by self-draw. Big hands only 清混碰 qīng hùn pèng: no plain wins, only flushes, all triplets, or both.

    總

    On one page

    • Goal. 4 sets + 1 pair = 14 tiles. A set is a run (3 in a row, same suit) or a triplet (3 identical).
    • Tiles. Dots, Bamboo, Characters, 1 to 9, four of each. Winds make triplets and pairs only. Flowers and, in Shanghai, dragons are bonus points: show them and draw again.
    • Your turn. Draw one, win if you can, otherwise discard one. Play moves to the right.
    • Taking discards. Pèng from anyone. Chī only from your left. Gàng is four of a kind; then draw a replacement. A win beats pung or kong, which beats chow.
    • Knock. One tile away, call qiāo. No knock, no win. After knocking, your hand is frozen.
    • Winning. Self-draw: all three others pay. Win on a discard: only the thrower pays.
    • Scoring. Score = fan × (1 + flowers), up to the cap. Purer hands pay more.
    問

    Check yourself

    Five questions. Tap one to see the answer.

    Is this a winning hand?

    Yes. Two runs, two triplets, one pair.

    How many sets is this?

    One set plus a spare tile.

    Everything in your hand is complete except the 4 and 5 of Bamboo. Which tiles win?

    The 3 or the 6 of Bamboo.

    The player opposite you throws the tile that completes your run. Can you chow it?

    No. Chow only from the player on your left.

    All triplets, 2 flowers, self-drawn. How much does each other player pay?

    2 × (1 + 2) = 6 points.
